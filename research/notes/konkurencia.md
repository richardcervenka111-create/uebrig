## Key Question 1 — Swiss players (who does what, counterparties, legal form, model, scale, Bern presence, funding, weaknesses)

### Takeaway
Switzerland has (a) one dominant B2C surplus-sale app (Too Good To Go, ~7'500 partners, 2.6 M registered users, 14 M bags saved, fee CHF 2.90/bag + CHF 59/year) plus one small Swiss challenger (GoNina, ~100 businesses, 25 % commission capped at CHF 2.50); (b) two national B2NGO/B2C-poor redistribution charities that move **retail** surplus by truck (Tischlein deck dich ~9'000 t/2025, 168 Abgabestellen, 8 in Stadt Bern; Schweizer Tafel ~8'500 t/2025, >500 institutions incl. Region Bern); (c) a volunteer/fridge layer (foodsharing ~35 Fairteiler in Bern district, Madame Frigo 8 fridges in Stadt Bern, HQ Bern); (d) commercial upcyclers (Äss-Bar, 2 shops in Bern). **None of them is built around same-evening transfer of freshly cooked restaurant surplus to social organisations** — see Key Question 3 and the gap analysis.

### Inferences
- The Swiss redistribution incumbents are **retail-to-charity trucking operations** (5-kg bags, pallets, fixed weekly Abgabestellen). Their unit is the tonne, their supplier is Coop/Migros/Aldi/Lidl, their cadence is weekly/bi-weekly. A restaurant's 12 portions of risotto at 21:30 does not fit any of their pipelines.
- TGTG's Swiss presence is thinning operationally (management moved to Vienna) while the product is pushed toward retail software; a Bern gastronomy partner gets a marketplace, not a donation channel to Sleeper.
- GoNina explicitly targets Bern and is the only other tech player with a Bern footprint — but it is B2C paid-bag, so it competes with Übrig **only for the restaurant's attention/surplus**, not for the NGO counterparty.
- No Bern organisation was found whose core is B2NGO same-day cooked-meal transfer; the Foodsave-Bankett proves demand and network exist (40 organisations) but runs once a year.

## Key Question 2 — International models worth copying or avoiding

### Takeaway
Three archetypes are visible: (1) **B2C paid surplus-bag marketplaces** (TGTG, Karma, ResQ, GoNina) — venture-funded, thin margins, several near-death experiences; (2) **retailer-paid charity redistribution platforms** (FoodCloud, FareShare Go, Olio Food Waste Heroes, Phenix) — the retailer pays because it is cheaper than disposal or legally required; (3) **US fee-for-service + tax-deduction rescue** (Copia, Goodr, Replate, RLC) — the donor pays a logistics fee and recoups via enhanced PATH-Act deductions. Only archetype 3 and the volunteer-driver nonprofits (RLC, Food Rescue US, 412 Food Rescue) routinely move **prepared** food from restaurants/caterers to shelters.

## Key Question 3 — Who transfers HOT, freshly cooked restaurant surplus the same evening, and how do they handle food safety?

### Takeaway
In Switzerland **no platform found does this systematically**; the two national charities handle packaged retail goods, TGTG/GoNina sell to consumers, fridges are C2C. The legal frame does exist: the 2021 BLV Spendenleitfaden explicitly allows prepared food to be passed on if kept ≥ +60 °C or cooled to < +10 °C within two hours, with donor **and** recipient each responsible as food business operators. The US volunteer nonprofits (RLC, Food Rescue US) and fee-for-service firms (Copia, Goodr) are the international models that do move prepared food same day.

### Cited Findings
- **Swiss rule (BLV/BAFU "Leitfaden zur Reduktion von Lebensmittelverlusten bei der Weitergabe", Beretta et al., 2021, revision of 2015 guide):** prepared foods must be handed on either **hot at ≥ +60 °C or chilled below +10 °C**, and when cooling **+10 °C must be reached within two hours**; covers fresh prepared foods, undated fruit/veg/bakery and mislabelled goods; **both donor and recipient organisation are food business operators and must each assure themselves of edibility** — [BAFU PDF](https://www.bafu.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/9HPcMiRiuAf0/2021_Beretta-etal_BLV-Spendenleitfaden.pdf); [fial.ch copy](https://www.fial.ch/dokumente/2021-blv-spendenleitfaden.pdf); [ZHAW copy](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/23509/3/2021_Beretta-etal_BLV-Spendenleitfaden.pdf); intended audience: manufacturers, distributors, recipients such as social institutions (same sources). BLV general page on responsibilities — [BLV](https://www.blv.admin.ch/blv/de/home/lebensmittel-und-ernaehrung/lebensmittelsicherheit/verantwortlichkeiten.html)
- Tischlein deck dich accepts "intact, still-edible food products"; goods that cannot legally be passed on go to biogas — [sustainableswitzerland.ch](https://sustainableswitzerland.ch/artikel/lidl-schweiz/tischlein-deck-dich-ist-ein-tolles-maerchen-aber-ein-solches-schlaraffenland-bieten-wir-nicht-id.1134) (no snippet found stating they accept cooked restaurant food).

- Foodsharing Bern Fairteiler are largely Madame Frigo fridges (C2C shelves/fridges) — [foodsharing Bern](https://foodsharing.de/fairteiler?bid=377); Bern fridges reported empty or with alcohol — [watson/BärnToday](https://www.watson.ch/bern/baerntoday/670286034-alkohol-oder-leere-das-steckt-in-den-oeffentlichen-kuehlschraenken-in-bern)

## Gap analysis — what is NOT covered in Bern (explicit)

### Inferences
1. **Uncovered niche = B2NGO, prepared food, same evening, small lots, reservation-based.** Nobody in Bern offers restaurants a *donation* button with a vetted counterparty and a hand-over protocol; the only "buttons" are sale (TGTG/GoNina) or an ungated fridge.
2. **Second uncovered niche: the hand-over record.** BLV makes both parties operators; no Bern tool produces the temperature/time/consume-by record that protects both sides. Übrig's label + log is the differentiator, not the listing.
3. **Third: canteens and caterers (SV Group, ZFV, Compass — all UAW members)** produce larger, more predictable evening surplus than à-la-carte restaurants and sit in Bern's Verwaltung/Uni/Insel; no B2NGO channel for them was found either.
4. **Risk of overlap:** if TGTG's "Donations" module reaches Swiss gastronomy (today retail-only) or GoNina adds a donate option, they could absorb the niche with existing merchant relationships. Übrig's defensible edge is the NGO network and local trust, not software.
5. **Monetisation reality check from comparables:** nobody makes the NGO pay; the kitchen pays only when it saves disposal cost or gets a tax lever. In Bern the realistic payer set is the city (Nachhaltige-Ernährung budget), foundations (SV Stiftung, church bodies), retailer/wholesaler CSR (Coop/Migros/Pistor/Transgourmet), or a per-lot "logistics contribution" later — free-for-all is fine for the pilot but has no comparable that sustained it beyond volunteer scale except foodsharing.
